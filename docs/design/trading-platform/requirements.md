## 1. How to read this document

- **IDs** run from R-001 to R-322 and are never reused or renumbered. A requirement that is dropped keeps its number and is marked withdrawn; five are (R-046, R-047, R-068, R-250, R-267). Requirements added on 2026-11-22 are R-289 to R-322, each at the end of its component's table. A requirement whose text was changed says so in its Source cell.
- **Source** uses these short forms: "Arch 5.3" is a section of `architecture.md`; "Arch D5", "Arch C4", "Arch gap 10" and "Arch Q6" are its decisions, conflicts, gaps and open questions; papers are cited as the architecture cites them (short title in italics, PDF page index). "I4" points to an item in section 5 of this document, where the architecture had to be interpreted. "N" marks a number chosen by this document and listed in section 3.
- **Acceptance check** is a pytest node id unless it is written as a command. It is relative to the test directory named above each table (a path starting with `tests/` is relative to the repository root). A check passes when `pytest <node id>` exits 0. The test names are part of the requirement: the plan stage must create them under these names. Several test files share a base name across directories (`test_perf.py`, `test_contract.py`), so the test tree must be set up to allow that.
- The **default checks** are four commands: `ruff check .`, `ruff format --check .`, `mypy src/atp` and a plain `pytest -q` (R-246 to R-249). Every acceptance check that is a pytest node id runs offline under the plain `pytest -q` unless it is marked otherwise.
- Checks marked **(goal)** are benchmarks. They run with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 pytest -m slow <node id>` on the owner's machine and are deselected by a plain `pytest -q`. Such a check passes when it writes the measured value, the goal, whether the goal was met and a description of the machine to `$ATP_HOME/bench/<test name>.json`; missing the goal does not fail it (RQ15, decided 2026-11-22).
- Checks marked **(alpaca)** need paper-account keys and network access and are deselected by a plain `pytest -q`.
- "Market-equivalent" volume or dollar volume is the value of R-292: the stored value for a SIP bar, scaled up for an IEX bar. Every liquidity measure uses it (R-049, R-102, R-128, R-140).
- "Synthetic panel" means data from the generator of R-018 to R-023. "Randomized after `as_of`" means every stored record whose `available_at` is later than `as_of` is replaced by random values, as in the look-ahead check of *LLM Trading Agents Earn Beta* p4.
- The papers were read from a plain-text extraction (no PDF renderer on this machine either). Prose is reliable; equations and tables are garbled. Where a check depends on a formula or a worked number, the row says what was legible and what must be confirmed against the PDF.

### Proposed risk limits

Proposed on 2026-11-22 in answer to RQ12 ("open to suggestions"), for the $100,000 long-only paper account assumed under Q5. They are a backstop behind a strategy's own constraints, not a strategy's settings. They are shipped in `configs/paper.toml` (R-313) and are confirmed by merging this amendment. Fields ending in `_pct` are percentages (R-305).

| Field | Proposed value | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `max_order_notional` | $10,000 | One order can build a 10% position at $100,000 of equity. It is an absolute amount: if equity grows, it binds before the weight limit and should be raised with it. |
| `max_position_weight` | 0.10 | Looser than a 500-name strategy's own weight bounds, and a ten-position ETF strategy still fits. |
| `max_gross_leverage` | 1.0 | No borrowing for a long-only account. |
| `max_net_leverage` | 1.0 | As above. |
| `max_daily_turnover` | 0.5 | By R-166's definition this allows building the whole book from cash in one batch (buys of 100% of equity are a turnover of 0.5) and blocks a full rotation in one day (1.0). |
| `max_orders` | 600 | The 500-name universe plus the ETF list, with about 10% headroom. |
| `price_collar_pct` | 20 | Catches an unadjusted split or a bad print. It also rejects, for one day, both buys and sells of a name that really moved more than 20%; that is accepted for a backstop and raises an alert (R-277). |
| `max_daily_loss_pct` | 5 | An ordinary bad day does not trip the kill switch; a broken strategy does. |
| `max_drawdown_pct` | 20 | As above over a longer period. A long-only strategy will reach it in a deep bear market, which stops trading until a person clears the switch; clearing resets the high-water mark (R-306). |
| `restricted` | empty | No name is restricted by default. |

**Quotes: what was checked on 2026-11-22 and what was not.** From Alpaca's documentation: historical quotes are served by `GET /v2/stocks/quotes`; `limit` is 1 to 10,000 (default 1,000) and "applies to the total number of data points, not per symbol"; results are "sorted by symbol first, then by the quote timestamp", so one symbol can fill a page of a multi-symbol request; `feed` defaults to `sip`. A quote has the fields `ap`, `as`, `ax`, `bp`, `bs`, `bx`, `c`, `t` and `z` (ask and bid price, size and exchange, conditions, timestamp, tape), and the response has `quotes` keyed by symbol, `currency` and `next_page_token`. The reference defines 403 as "The requested resource is forbidden, for example because the subscription does not cover the requested data"; the FAQ's example of a refusal is `{"code":42210000,"message":"subscription does not permit querying recent SIP data"}`, and no page says which status carries it. The free plan allows 200 calls per minute and history since 2016 except the latest 15 minutes, and the FAQ says "the `end` parameter must be at least 15 minutes old to query SIP data without a subscription". The paper-trading page says a paper-only account is "only entitled to receive and make use of IEX market data". Quotes are tick data, so a complete history for 500 names is far beyond 200 pages a minute; that is why R-296 to R-298 sample one bounded window per name and session. Not verified, and to be recorded by R-190 before the code relies on it: whether a paper-only account can read SIP quotes at all (the two pages disagree, as they do for bars); what exactly the response is when a feed is not covered, which is why R-290 names the meaning and not a status code; how far back quotes reach on either feed; whether the 200-call limit is shared between endpoints (R-043 assumes it is); and how much wider single-venue IEX quotes are than the national best bid and offer, which would overstate spreads on IEX data.

Three statements in the architecture could not be turned into a requirement because nothing observable follows from them: that trials should be planned in advance (*Deflated Sharpe Ratio* p10), that PBO must never become an optimization target (covered only by having no search command, section 6), and that an LLM is treated as a signal source and not as the edge.

## 6. Out of scope

- Live trading: no live endpoint, no configuration switch, no `live` promotion state.
- Tick, order-book and market-making strategies; level-2 data.
- Ingestion of minute bars and any strategy on them. Only the engine seam is required (R-023, R-146).
- A long-running or streaming runner. Jobs are one-shot commands.
- Multi-period optimization, Almgren-Chriss execution schedules and the linear impact model.
- Reinforcement-learning policies, deep networks, time-series foundation models.
- Fundamental or accounting data, the 94-characteristic set, a second data vendor.
- Complete tick-level quote history, intraday spread curves and quotes at the open. Quotes are sampled once per name and session (R-296).
- The sector-ETF variant of residual reversion, a sector classification, and a variable number of PCA factors (RQ8).
- Running two strategies in one paper account at the same time. One configuration holds the account, and another can only take it over (R-311, R-322).
- An autonomous or multi-agent LLM trader, and automated factor mining.
- Any command or API that searches or tunes configurations automatically, and any use of PBO to select a configuration.
- A second open-source backtesting engine as a cross-check.
- Calibrating the impact coefficient from fills (paper trading simulates no impact).
- Corporate actions other than splits and cash dividends in the ledger (mergers, spin-offs, stock dividends, worthless removals) beyond the close-out of R-142. They are stored and reported by the data check only.
- Hard-to-borrow short sales and locates.
- Taxes, wash sales, regulatory fees and day-trading rules.
- Dividends in the paper-trading comparison (Alpaca paper trading pays none).
- A web interface or dashboard.
- Delivery of alerts to a person, and installation of a scheduler (RQ18).
- A concrete LLM provider adapter (RQ17).
- Changes to `.devteam/`, `.github/workflows/`, `REVIEW.md` and `docs/reference/`, which agents cannot edit. A human deletes `.devteam/verify.sh` and removes its step from the workflow (RQ1, decided 2026-11-22).
